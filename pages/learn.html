Before you start — the 3 things you need

01

Binary: everything is 0 or 1

Digital circuits think in two voltage levels. We call them 0 (LOW, ~0 V) and 1 (HIGH, the supply voltage ≈ 5 V or 3.3 V). Every gate takes 0s and 1s in, and produces a 0 or a 1 out. That's the whole game.

02

How a truth table works

A truth table lists every possible input combination and the output for each. A 2-input gate has 2² = 4 rows. Read it like a menu: pick the row that matches your inputs, and the Y column tells you the answer.

03

The three boolean symbols

  • · is AND — read "dot" (A · B = A and B)
  • + is OR — read "plus" (A + B = A or B)
  • ′ or a bar is NOT — read "prime/bar" (A′ = not A)

02

Reading boolean expressions

Expressions combine gates. In Y = A · B + C read it as (A AND B) OR C — the · binds tighter than the +, just like × binds tighter than + in arithmetic.
⊕ and equality. Y = A ⊕ B means XOR ("exclusive or" — different). Y = (A ⊕ B)′ is XNOR (equal). A prime right after a bracket means invert the whole thing.

04

De Morgan's Theorems

The single best exam tool in this whole course
De Morgan lets you flip AND ↔ OR by inverting inputs and output. Inverting the inputs and the output of a circuit never wastes anything — it just changes the gate.
THEOREM 1

(A · B)′ = A′ + B′

A NAND behaves exactly like an OR of two NOTs. "Not (A and B) is the same as (not A) or (not B)."

THEOREM 2

(A + B)′ = A′ · B′

A NOR behaves exactly like an AND of two NOTs. "Not (A or B) is the same as (not A) and (not B)."

Why it matters: because of De Morgan, you never need an AND chip AND an OR chip — NAND and NOR alone can make any circuit. Chips love it because fewer gate types means cheaper hardware.
05

Universal gates: build anything with NAND (or NOR)

N

Everything from NAND

  • NOT — tie both inputs together: NAND(A, A) = A′
  • AND — NAND then NOT: (A · B)′ ′ = A · B
  • OR — De Morgan: NAND(A′, B′) = A + B
N

Everything from NOR

  • NOT — tie both inputs: NOR(A, A) = A′
  • OR — NOR then NOT: (A + B)′ ′ = A + B
  • AND — De Morgan: NOR(A′, B′) = A · B
Challenge: open the Circuit Builder and build a real XOR using only NAND gates. It takes four — a famous puzzle, and the answer is everywhere online if you get stuck.

Putting it together: the half adder

Add two bits · the half adder

APPLICATION

Adding two 1-bit numbers needs two outputs: the sum and the carry. Two gates do it all: XOR = sum, AND = carry.

0000
0110
1010

➗

Why XOR = sum and AND = carry?

  • 0+0 = 00 → sum 0, carry 0
  • 1+0 = 01 → sum 1, carry 0
  • 1+1 = 10 → sum 0, carry 1 (this is how "2" is written in binary!)

The sum is 1 exactly when inputs differ → that's XOR. The carry is 1 only when both are 1 → that's AND.

Exam glossary — know these cold

BitThe smallest unit of digital data — a 0 or a 1.
Truth tableLists every input combination and its output.
Boolean expressionAlgebra that describes a gate with ·, +, ′.
Active HIGH / LOWA signal that means "on" as 1, or as 0.
NAND / NORUniversal gates — any circuit can be built from them.
De MorganFlipping AND↔OR via inversion of inputs & output.
XORInequality comparator and half-adder sum bit.
XNOREquality comparator; the XOR's complement.
Half adderAdds two bits → sum (XOR) and carry (AND).
Logic levelThe voltage meaning of a bit: LOW ≈ 0 V, HIGH ≈ Vcc.
Flip-flopA memory cell made from NAND/NOR gates that holds a bit.
Propagation delayThe tiny time a real gate takes to output its answer.
